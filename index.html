Same angle, same ratios

Stand at the angle θ. The side across from you is the opposite, the side touching you is the adjacent, and the longest side (across from the right angle) is the hypotenuse.

sin θ = O ÷ H   cos θ = A ÷ H   tan θ = O ÷ A

Change the size: the ratios don't move. Change the angle: they do. That is why a calculator can store "sin 35°".

Press "Stand at the other angle": opposite and adjacent swap, so sin of one angle equals cos of the other. Those are cofunctions: sin θ = cos(90° − θ).

Three more ratios: flip the first three

csc θ = 1 ÷ sin θ = H ÷ O  ·  sec θ = 1 ÷ cos θ = H ÷ A  ·  cot θ = 1 ÷ tan θ = A ÷ O

Each pair has exactly one "co": sin with cosec, cos with sec. Calculators have no csc button: csc 40° means 1 ÷ sin 40°.

Example 1: a ladder (find a side)

A 5 m ladder leans on a wall at 70° to the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?

  1. Sketch it. The ladder is the hypotenuse (5 m). The height on the wall is opposite the 70° angle.
  2. You have H and want O, so use SOH: sin 70° = h ÷ 5.
  3. Multiply both sides by 5: h = 5 × sin 70° = 5 × 0.940 ≈ 4.70 m.
  4. Check: a leg is always shorter than the hypotenuse, and 4.70 < 5 ✓

Example 2: a ramp (find an angle)

A ramp rises 1 m over 12 m along the ground. What angle does it make with the ground?

  1. The rise (1 m) is opposite the angle; the 12 m along the ground is adjacent.
  2. O and A means TOA: tan θ = 1 ÷ 12 ≈ 0.0833.
  3. To get the angle back, use the inverse button: θ = tan⁻¹(0.0833) ≈ 4.8°.
  4. Check: a gentle ramp should have a small angle ✓

Two triangles that give exact answers

Don't memorise a table. Rebuild these two in 10 seconds and read any value off them with SOH CAH TOA.

Half a square: legs 1 and 1, hypotenuse √2. Both small angles are 45°.

Half an equilateral triangle: sides 2, cut in half: base 1, hypotenuse 2, height √(4 − 1) = √3. Angles 30°, 60°, 90°.

The shortest side is always opposite the smallest angle: 1 is opposite 30°.

Past 90°: sine is a height

Swing an arm of length 1 from the right-hand axis. Its height is sin θ and its sideways distance is cos θ.

Mirror the arm across the vertical axis: the angle becomes 180° − θ, the height stays the same, and the sideways distance flips sign.

sin(180° − θ) = sin θ    cos(180° − θ) = −cos θ

So if sin B = 0.5, B could be 30° or 150°. Remember this: it causes the ambiguous case below.

The sine law: pairs that match

a ÷ sin A = b ÷ sin B = c ÷ sin C

Label first: side a faces angle A, side b faces B, side c faces C. Each side and the angle across from it make a pair, and every pair gives the same number.

You need one complete pair to start. Then the sine law finds a side (from two angles and a side) or an angle (from two sides and an angle not between them).

Finding an angle? Flip it so the unknown is on top: sin B ÷ b = sin A ÷ a.

Example 3: find a side

In △ABC, A = 40°, B = 60° and a = 10 cm. Find b.

  1. Complete pair: a = 10 with A = 40°. We want b, which pairs with B = 60°.
  2. Set up: b ÷ sin 60° = 10 ÷ sin 40°.
  3. Multiply by sin 60°: b = 10 × sin 60° ÷ sin 40° = 10 × 0.866 ÷ 0.643.
  4. b ≈ 13.5 cm.
  5. Check: the bigger angle faces the bigger side. 60° > 40° and 13.5 > 10 ✓ (C = 180° − 40° − 60° = 80°, if you need c next.)

Example 4: two answers

A = 30°, b = 10, a = 6. How many triangles? Find B.

  1. Height: h = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 0.5 = 5.
  2. Compare: 5 < 6 < 10, so a is between h and b: two triangles.
  3. Sine law: sin B = 10 × sin 30° ÷ 6 = 5 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.833.
  4. Acute answer: B = sin⁻¹(0.833) ≈ 56.4°.
  5. Obtuse answer: B = 180° − 56.4° = 123.6°. Check the third angle is positive: 180° − 30° − 123.6° = 26.4° ✓

Side b is fixed at 10. Try a = 4, then 5, then 6, then 12 with A = 30°.

The cosine law: Pythagoras with a correction

c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

Open the angle C between two fixed sides. At exactly 90°, cos C = 0 and the correction disappears: plain Pythagoras. Narrower, and c is shorter than Pythagoras; wider, and c is longer.

Use it when there is no complete pair: two sides and the angle between them (SAS), or all three sides (SSS).

Example 5: SAS, no calculator

a = 5, b = 7 and the angle between them is C = 60°. Find c.

  1. c² = 5² + 7² − 2(5)(7) cos 60°
  2. cos 60° = 1/2, so the correction is 2 × 5 × 7 × 1/2 = 35.
  3. c² = 25 + 49 − 35 = 39, so c = √39 ≈ 6.24.
  4. Watch out: do 2ab cos C as one product. Never work out (a² + b² − 2ab) first and then multiply.

Example 6: SSS, find the largest angle

The sides are 3, 5 and 7. Find the largest angle.

  1. The largest angle faces the longest side, so call that side c = 7.
  2. Rearrange: cos C = (a² + b² − c²) ÷ 2ab = (9 + 25 − 49) ÷ 30.
  3. cos C = −15 ÷ 30 = −0.5.
  4. C = cos⁻¹(−0.5) = 120°. The negative cosine told you it would be obtuse.

1. A triangle has sides 20, 30 and 40 m. Which tool finds the largest angle, and what is it?

Cosine law (SSS). The largest angle faces 40: cos C = (400 + 900 − 1600) ÷ 1200 = −0.25, so C ≈ 104.5°.

2. Without a calculator: sin 60° × cos 30° = ?

(√3/2) × (√3/2) = 3/4.

3. Rewrite sec 25° using its cofunction.

csc 65°, because 25° + 65° = 90°.

h = 20 sin 40° ≈ 12.9. Since 12.9 < 15 < 20, there are two triangles.
